## Purpose and boundaries

This inventory translates the requested marketplace into a proposed delivery scope. Everything in the module and panel tables is **PROPOSED**, not a claim about the reference business. Phase assignments are sequencing hypotheses, not promises. The first release is the managed seller-to-dealer transaction loop, including human operating controls; later phases add consumer and advanced services. A jurisdiction, seller and buyer obligations, custody of money, fees, and contract form require decisions and qualified legal review before production transactions. Platform-owned lending, escrow, vehicle ownership, and warranty obligations are not presumed.

Phase key: **1** foundational managed loop; **2** commercial marketplace; **3** advanced operations; **4** differentiation. A capability marked with multiple phases begins with a minimum manual or assisted form and expands later. A phase 1 entry does not imply that optional commercial or legal policies have been approved.

## Proposed role and panel inventory

Panels are logical permission and workflow boundaries, not necessarily 20 separate deployments. The recommended default is a shared operations application with strongly scoped modules; public/seller and dealer experiences are separate responsive web frontends sharing trusted services. Dedicated native applications are deferred until field research demonstrates an offline or device-specific need. Sensitive capabilities require explicit grants, object/branch scope and auditable actions; named roles below are illustrative bundles, not automatic permission inheritance.

| ID / panel | Primary users and supported workflow | Recommended surface | Sensitive permission or boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| PANEL-01 Public website | Visitors discover services, branches, help and legal information; enter selling or buying flow. | Public web | No private auction data or unpublished inventory; public claims require editorial approval. |
| PANEL-02 Seller dashboard | Seller submits vehicle, books, tracks inspection and chooses an offer. | Customer web | Seller sees only their own cases and documents; decision requires authenticated identity. |

## End-to-end journeys and recovery

These are discovery journeys, not final state machines or API contracts. Every irreversible external step remains subject to the selected business model and relevant approvals.

| Journey | Normal progression and responsible users | Exceptions and recovery | Evidence and access boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seller to auction and closure | Seller submits vehicle and consent → branch books and checks in → inspector records, supervisor releases report → analyst reviews valuation → eligible dealer auction runs → seller receives result and accepts or rejects → legal coordinates documents → finance records settlement → staff record transfer/handover and close. | Invalid identity or missing documents pauses case for review; no slot offers alternative branch/time; no-show permits rescheduling; disputed inspection creates reviewed revision; low confidence goes to analyst; no qualifying result or seller rejection closes as unsuccessful or enters a separately approved relisting process; payment/transfer failure leaves case open with owner and deadline. | Seller accesses own case; staff use branch/assignment scope; signed decision, report revision, bid outcome, contract and settlement evidence must remain auditable. |
| Dealer onboarding and bidding | Dealer representative submits business identity → reviewer approves organization and authorized bidders → bidder views eligible report and places bid → service returns accepted/rejected result → auction closes → winning organization receives permitted next steps. | Failed verification stays pending or rejected with appeal path; suspended dealer cannot bid; stale price, insufficient increment, deadline expiry, lost connection or duplicate submission returns authoritative outcome without creating a second valid bid; ambiguous acknowledgement is reconciled from server history before retry; default escalates under approved rules. | Dealer sees only eligible auctions and own organization history; auction staff cannot silently change accepted bid history; all final outcomes are based on server-side decisions. |
| Consumer discovery and purchase inquiry | Buyer filters consumer-publishable inventory, inspects details and submits inquiry or appointment request → advisor confirms availability and coordinates next steps. | Vehicle becomes unavailable: mark listing accordingly, retain inquiry and inform buyer; invalid contact or failed message prompts retry or agent follow-up; reservation is unavailable until policy is approved. | Only published consumer data is public; buyer case is visible to assigned staff and the authenticated buyer where supported. |
| Custom sourcing | Buyer states attributes, location and budget → advisor qualifies request and proposes candidate → buyer gives feedback → selected candidate is inspected and purchase is coordinated. | No suitable candidate triggers a scheduled review or closure by agreement; buyer may cancel; price changes require renewed buyer acknowledgement. | Buyer request and contacts are scoped to assigned advisor and the buyer; acceptance must be evidenced. |
| Contract, settlement and transfer | Authorized staff select reviewed agreement and coordinate signatures → finance tracks obligations using approved money-flow policy → independent reconciliation verifies evidence → legal records ownership-transfer status → operations confirms handover. | Signature refusal, mismatch, payment failure or reconciliation discrepancy blocks closure; assign owner, preserve evidence and escalate without marking funds settled on an unverified assertion. Refund or compensation follows approved authorization and legal policy. | Contract and payment documents are restricted; finance reconciliation approval and legal transfer evidence are separately recorded. |
| Support, inspection dispute or warranty claim | Customer opens issue → support assigns and acknowledges → authorized reviewer collects evidence and assesses applicable terms → authorized decision-maker records decision, remedy and notification. | Missing evidence sets follow-up deadline; conflict or contested outcome goes to a separate reviewer; unresolved complaints stay open and escalate against an approved SLA; denied claims retain appeal instructions. | Participants see only appropriate information; evidence access, decision rationale and any remedy approval are logged. |
| Financing referral | Buyer consents to share required information → authorized provider assesses application → provider communicates decision and terms → operations coordinates documented funding if approved. | Consent withdrawal stops further sharing where legally possible; rejection or expiry offers non-financing options where available; provider outage is pending, not approval. | Provider, not the platform UI, determines its approval; minimize and retain data according to approved policy. |
| Delivery and custody | Authorized staff schedule carrier → handover condition and custody recorded → carrier transports → recipient signs proof of delivery → case updated. | Delay notifies participants; missed delivery is rescheduled with custody tracked; damage preserves before/after evidence and routes to claims; no proof means delivery remains unconfirmed. | Carrier sees assigned transfer only; transfer of physical custody and legal ownership are distinct milestones. |

## Discovery decisions, risks and next specifications

- **DECISION REQUIRED — transaction model:** select intermediary, inventory ownership or another model per jurisdiction; assign buyer/seller obligations, fee payer and transfer responsibility. Until approved, settlement is external status tracking only and implementation readiness is blocked for production transaction flows.
- **DECISION REQUIRED — funds:** select legally reviewed direct settlement, provider-mediated settlement, approved escrow, or authorized platform collection/disbursement; define refunds, reconciliation, who holds money, failure and dispute liability. Multiple models may be supported as explicitly configured policies, not active defaults.
- **DECISION REQUIRED — auction rules:** approve bidder eligibility, binding effect, seller response window, reserve visibility, close and tie behavior, cancellation, default and any optional extensions, deposits or negotiation. This discovery document does not set financial or legal auction rules.
- **DECISION REQUIRED — jurisdiction:** determine licensing, consent, retention, tax, electronic signature, ownership transfer and warranty commitments with qualified local counsel. English language does not select a market.
- **PROPOSED:** use human-reviewed valuations and manually coordinated contracts and settlement in phase 1. Validate staffing and operational throughput before promising automation.
- **UNVERIFIED:** staffing volume, target transaction rates, external provider capabilities, inspection accuracy, customer demand and the measurable costs of each operational stage require discovery.
- **Outstanding specifications:** complete personas, navigation and every screen state; scoped permission matrix; service state machines and guard conditions; contracts and event semantics; accounting and legal policies; acceptance tests, metrics and operational SLAs. This inventory does not replace them or claim implementation readiness.

## Batch consistency check

The foundational loop has identified actors, dependencies and failure paths; precise data/API contracts and acceptance criteria remain outstanding. A consumer listing is distinct from a dealer auction, an inspection is not a warranty, and custody of a vehicle is not proof of legal ownership or payment. Optional auction, lending and payment models remain explicitly unapproved. Panel names describe logical surfaces; the future architecture may consolidate them without relaxing authorization. No reference-business facts are asserted in this document.
