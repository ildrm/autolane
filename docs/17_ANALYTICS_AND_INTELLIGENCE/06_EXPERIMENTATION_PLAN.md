# Experimentation plan

**ID:** 17/06_EXPERIMENTATION_PLAN · **Version:** 0.1 · **Status:** PROPOSED, subject to privacy, finance and product approval · **Owner:** Head of data · **Reviewers:** Analytics, security, legal, finance, product · **Updated:** 2026-11-15

## Experiment contract
Each experiment has a preregistered hypothesis, eligible population, unit of randomization, start/end, primary metric, guardrails, minimum detectable effect, sample-size calculation, sequential monitoring rule, owner and decision date. Randomize at the person or organization level where repeat exposure could contaminate outcomes; persistent assignment only when legal basis permits. If identity cannot be used, run a consented or cluster-level alternative and report limits. Avoid exposing unconsented users to optional tracking or processing.

## Example hypotheses
| Experiment | Primary outcome / eligibility | Guardrails and stop |
|---|---|---|
| Seller form: three steps vs one long form | Qualified submitted leads per eligible consented unique starter, matured cohort. | No increase in misleading submissions, privacy complaints or accessibility failures; pause on unsafe promise or broken booking. |
| Inspection explanation: finding list plus diagram vs list only | Correct answer rate for interpretation in moderated study; later eligible report engagement. | Actual defect evidence remains identical; no change to report or auction fairness. |
| Dealer search defaults | Eligible organizations with a meaningful auction view per randomized organization. | No degradation to bid acknowledgment, fairness, latency or accessibility. |

## Analysis and governance
Power calculations require a measured baseline; no duration, sample or uplift is asserted now. Use intention-to-treat, report assignment, sample-ratio mismatch, exclusions, missingness, confidence intervals and operational changes. Avoid uncontrolled repeated significance checks; define any sequential method in advance. Financial terms, reserve visibility, eligibility and bid ordering are never randomized without legal, commercial, security and auction fairness approval. Do not infer causality from observational before/after data. Product owns decisions, analyst owns design/analysis, privacy and accessibility approve data/experience, and experiment logs preserve versions, results and rollback.
