# UX research plan

**ID:** 05/01_UX_RESEARCH_PLAN · **Version:** 0.1 · **Status:** PROPOSED, subject to research and stakeholder review · **Owner:** UX lead · **Reviewers:** Product, accessibility, operations, engineering, legal · **Updated:** 2026-11-15

## Purpose and evidence
Establish whether sellers, dealers, inspectors and operations staff can understand and complete a transaction. Reference research in [the evidence register](../00_RESEARCH_EVIDENCE.md) describes public claims; no user interview or usability result is represented as completed. The personas and assumptions below are hypotheses, not observed findings.

## Hypotheses and research questions
| ID | Hypothesis | Risk if wrong | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|---|---|
| UH-01 | Sellers value a clear conditional offer and physical inspection more than a promised instant price. | Misleading claims, abandonment. | Ask what counts as a binding offer; observe explanation recall. |
| UH-02 | Dealers need report revision, current price and reliable deadline together before bidding. | Loss of trust or invalid bids. | Ask participants to explain report version and what happens on connection loss. |
| UH-03 | Inspectors can complete a checklist while moving around a car on mobile. | Missing defects and inaccurate reports. | Observe gloved use, camera capture, interrupted network and supervisor corrections. |
| UH-04 | Staff can separate auction result, seller approval, payment verification and ownership transfer. | Premature closure. | Scenario-sort the four milestones and resolve a discrepancy. |
| UH-05 | Asking for only the minimum vehicle attributes before booking increases qualified starts. | Low-quality leads or privacy objection. | Compare alternative prototypes; measure completion and data corrections. |

## Recruitment and method
Recruit 8 recent sellers, 6 professional buyers, 4 inspectors and 4 branch/finance staff for moderated 45–60 minute interviews; include mobile-only, low-vision, keyboard/screen-reader and limited-connectivity participants. This is a proposed qualitative sample, not statistically representative. Recruit through independent channels, compensate consistently, document consent, avoid collecting real financial, identity or vehicle documents. Run an initial discovery round then 5 task-based sessions per main role on revised prototype; add targeted participants when critical failure recurs. Local privacy and retention review are required before recruitment.

## Interview and usability protocol
Start with a recent real experience, without prompting brand preference. Ask: “How did you decide whom to trust?”, “What would make you stop?”, “What did you need to know before agreeing?”, “What happened when an expected payment or appointment changed?” For staff ask who may correct a report and how disagreement is escalated. Tasks: submit a seller request, book/rebook a slot, read an inspection, place a bid after price changes, reject an offer, identify payment and transfer status, and raise a dispute. Give scenario goals, not UI instructions; record success, time, assistance, errors and participant explanation. For the dealer, simulate a disconnected acknowledgement and require history reconciliation before retry. Stop whenever distressed; never submit real bids.

## Analysis, ethics and acceptance
Code anonymized notes by task and severity: critical = cannot complete or makes unsafe commitment; major = needs assistance; moderate = delayed; minor = cosmetic. Record denominator, device, prototype version and confidence; avoid claiming causation from five sessions. Store recordings only with explicit consent, restrict access and delete under approved schedule. A usability gate requires zero unresolved critical issues in seller decision, bidding or settlement comprehension; a participant with assistive technology must complete each relevant task unaided or the issue is escalated. Product and research approve an action owner, fix and retest for each critical finding. Consent, participant access and final compensation are **DECISION REQUIRED**.
